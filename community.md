# BikeTag Community Design Project

## Building BikeTag Together

BikeTag is a community-driven bicycle photo-tagging game. Each local game has its own personality, players, and identity.

We're inviting the community to help shape the next generation of BikeTag.org.

The goal is to create a modern, flexible website design that can be shared across BikeTag games while allowing individual communities to express their own identities.

This is an open collaboration led by the BikeTag Project and this repository owner. Designers, artists, developers, ambassadors, and players are all welcome.

## What We're Working Toward

The project has two broad goals.

### 1. A New BikeTag Website Design

Develop a refreshed visual direction and website experience for BikeTag.

This includes:
- Website layouts for desktop and mobile.
- Navigation and page structure.
- Game branding and city identity.
- Current tag presentation.
- Previous tags and game history.
- Tag submission and player interactions.

### 2. Customizable BikeTag Themes

Develop a reusable design system that allows individual BikeTag games to customize their websites.

For example, a BikeTag game in one city might prefer a colorful, hand-drawn aesthetic, while another might prefer something more minimal.

We want to explore how communities could customize elements such as:

- Colors and typography.
- Illustrations and artwork.
- City branding and identity.
- Decorative elements and backgrounds.
- Selected visual components.

The goal is to balance a recognizable BikeTag experience with the freedom for local communities to make the game their own.

## How the Community Participates

There is no single correct way to contribute.

Some people will create polished mockups. Others will share rough sketches, write code, create illustrations, or simply tell us what they think.

All of these contributions are valuable.

GitHub is the central home for the project. It allows us to preserve ideas, organize discussions, track changes, and recognize contributors.

However, people can also participate through Patreon or email.

See [CONTRIBUTING.md](contributing.md) for practical instructions.

## How Ideas Become Part of BikeTag

We want to encourage experimentation rather than require everyone to agree on a single design from the beginning.

Our general process is:

1. **Explore:** Community members share ideas, concepts, artwork, and suggestions.
2. **Discuss:** Other contributors and players provide feedback.
3. **Develop:** Promising ideas are refined into more complete designs or working implementations.
4. **Review:** The repository owner review contributions and consider community feedback.
5. **Incorporate:** Selected work is integrated into the project's design direction and repository.

Not every contribution needs to progress through every stage. A useful suggestion or piece of artwork can be valuable on its own.

## Feedback and Design Decisions

We want community feedback to meaningfully influence the direction of BikeTag.

Discussions should be open, constructive, and focused on the goals of the project.

The repository owner will coordinate the initiative and make final decisions about what is incorporated into the official project.

This doesn't mean every decision needs to originate with the repository owner. We actively encourage community members to propose ideas, challenge assumptions, and help shape the direction.

When significant decisions are made, we'll make an effort to document:

- What was decided.
- Why the decision was made.
- Which community ideas or contributions influenced it.
- Whether the decision affects future customization or development.

Community feedback is important, but participation does not guarantee that every proposal will be adopted.

## Recognition and Attribution

We want the people who help build BikeTag to be recognized.

Contributors who work directly in the GitHub repository can receive recognition through GitHub's contributor history and commit records.

We also want to acknowledge artists, designers, ambassadors, and community members whose contributions don't involve code.

Contributors may be credited in project documentation, alongside their artwork, or in community announcements.

If you contribute through Patreon or email, we'll work with you to make sure your contribution can be appropriately documented and credited.

## Participate Without GitHub

You do not need a GitHub account or technical experience to participate.

**Patreon**

Our Patreon community will provide a place for people to share ideas, feedback, and creative contributions.

Participation is open to people using Patreon’s free supporter level. There is no requirement to become a paying member.

**Email**

You can also send ideas, designs, illustrations, and feedback directly to the BikeTag Project by email.

We'll help organize relevant contributions in the public GitHub repository.

Where appropriate, we'll ask contributors whether they're comfortable having their work and feedback shared publicly.

## Our Approach

We want this project to be approachable, collaborative, and fun.

There are no professional qualifications required, no expectation that contributors commit to a particular amount of time, and no requirement to agree with the repository owner or other contributors on every design decision.

Bring an idea. Share something you've made. Tell us what you think.

The best BikeTag experience will come from the people who make BikeTag what it is: its communities and players.

**Let's build it together.** 🚲
