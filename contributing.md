# Contributing to BikeTag

First off, thank you for your interest in helping shape the future of BikeTag!

BikeTag is an open-source bicycle photo-tagging game played by communities around the world. We're working together to develop a new visual direction for BikeTag.org and, eventually, a flexible design system that individual BikeTag games can customize for their own communities.

This is a community project, and everyone is welcome to participate. You don't need to be a professional designer, illustrator, or developer. You don't even need to know how to use GitHub.

Whether you have a complete website concept, an idea for a city-specific illustration, a suggestion for improving the player experience, or simply some feedback, we'd love to hear from you.

## Ways to Contribute

There are many ways to participate.

### 🎨 Designers

Help us explore the visual direction of BikeTag.

- Create website mockups and visual concepts.
- Experiment with desktop and mobile layouts.
- Explore colors, typography, and visual styles.
- Suggest improvements to the player experience.
- Develop reusable design patterns and components.

You can submit anything from a rough sketch to a polished design.

### 🖌️ Illustrators and Artists

Help give BikeTag its personality.

- Create illustrations and decorative artwork.
- Develop city-specific graphics and visual identities.
- Explore icons, badges, stickers, and other assets.
- Create artwork that individual games can customize.

Hand-drawn, digital, experimental, and unconventional styles are all welcome.

### 💻 Developers

Help turn community ideas into working designs.

- Build Vue components.
- Contribute CSS and styling.
- Develop reusable layouts and themes.
- Experiment with responsive designs.
- Help implement and refine the design system.

You don't need to take on a large project. Small improvements are valuable too.

### 🚲 BikeTag Ambassadors

Help represent your local BikeTag community.

- Suggest local colors, themes, and visual identities.
- Share ideas for city-specific artwork.
- Explain what makes your local game unique.
- Gather feedback from local players.
- Help us understand how a customizable BikeTag experience could work.

### 💬 Players and Community Members

You don't need to create anything to participate.

- Share what you like about BikeTag.
- Suggest improvements to the website.
- Tell us what is confusing or difficult to use.
- Comment on proposed designs.
- Help us understand what makes the game enjoyable.

Your experience playing BikeTag is valuable design input.

## Getting Started

The easiest way to get started is to explore the project and see what others are working on.

1. Visit this repository, you've already completed step 1!
2. Read this guide and explore the existing designs and discussions.
3. Look through open issues to see where help is needed.
4. Share an idea, ask a question, or start working on something that interests you.

You don't need permission to start exploring ideas.

If you're unsure where to begin, introduce yourself in a discussion and tell us what you'd like to contribute.

## Sharing Ideas and Designs

We encourage contributors to share work early and often. You don't need a finished design before asking for feedback.

Depending on what you're working on, you can:

- Open a GitHub issue describing your idea.
- Start a GitHub discussion to explore a concept.
- Attach screenshots, sketches, mockups, or reference images.
- Share links to Figma files or other design tools.
- Submit artwork or design files through a pull request.
- Send your contribution through Patreon or email.

When sharing a design, it helps to include a little context:

- What are you proposing?
- What problem or opportunity does it address?
- Is it intended for desktop, mobile, or both?
- Is it a general BikeTag design or intended for a particular city?
- Are there any details you'd like feedback on?

Don't worry about answering every question. Share what you have.

## GitHub Issues and Discussions

GitHub is where we organize the project and keep track of ideas.

**Issues** are useful for specific suggestions, problems, and pieces of work.

Examples:
- Suggest a new website layout.
- Propose a new visual component.
- Report a usability problem.
- Suggest a city-specific customization.
- Volunteer to work on a particular feature.

**Discussions** are better for open-ended conversations.

Examples:
- Explore different visual directions.
- Share inspiration and references.
- Ask questions about the project.
- Discuss how customizable themes should work.
- Give feedback on proposed designs.

You don't need to have a solution before opening an issue or discussion. Starting a conversation is a contribution in itself.

## Submitting Pull Requests

If you're comfortable working with GitHub, you can submit your work directly to the repository.

A pull request (PR) proposes changes that can be reviewed and incorporated into the project.

A typical contribution looks like this:

1. Fork the repository.
2. Create a branch for your changes.
3. Make your changes or add your design assets.
4. Commit your work with a descriptive message.
5. Open a pull request explaining what you've contributed.
6. Respond to feedback and make any requested adjustments.

Include screenshots or previews when contributing visual changes.

For larger changes, we recommend opening an issue or discussion first so you can get feedback before investing too much time.

**You don't have to submit perfect work.** Pull requests are a place to collaborate, improve ideas, and learn from one another.

If you're unfamiliar with Git or pull requests, you can still participate through discussions, Patreon, or email.

## Reviewing Contributions

The two BikeTag founders will coordinate the community design initiative and review contributions.

We want the review process to be collaborative and constructive.

When reviewing contributions, we'll consider:

- How well the contribution supports the goals of BikeTag.
- Its usability across desktop and mobile devices.
- How it could work for different BikeTag communities.
- Its potential for reuse and customization.
- Feedback from players, designers, developers, and ambassadors.

We may ask questions, suggest changes, or invite contributors to develop an idea further.

Not every suggestion will become part of the final design. Some ideas may be combined, revisited later, or used as inspiration for other work.

We'll make an effort to explain significant design decisions and keep discussions open.

## Recognition

We believe people deserve credit for the work they contribute.

Whenever possible, we encourage contributions directly through GitHub. Contributors who submit accepted pull requests will appear in the repository's contribution history, creating a public record of their involvement.

We also want to recognize contributions that don't take the form of code.

Depending on the contribution, recognition may include:

- Credit in project documentation.
- Attribution alongside artwork or design assets.
- Recognition in project announcements.
- Acknowledgment in the community's contributor history.

Contributions submitted through Patreon or email can receive recognition too. If you would like your work attributed publicly, please include the name or handle you'd like us to use.

## Contributing Without GitHub

GitHub is our preferred place to organize and preserve project contributions, but it isn't a requirement.

You can also participate through:

**Patreon**

We'll maintain a public community participation space on Patreon. You can share ideas, provide feedback, and submit contributions there.

A free Patreon supporter account is sufficient to participate. No paid membership is required.

**Email**

You can also email the BikeTag founders with your ideas, designs, artwork, or suggestions.

Please include a description of your contribution and how you'd like to be credited.

We'll help bring appropriate contributions into the public project repository so they can be discussed, reviewed, and preserved.

## Copyright and Licensing

Please only submit artwork, designs, photographs, code, and other materials that you created yourself or have permission to contribute.

You retain responsibility for ensuring that you have the rights necessary to share your work.

The project's final licensing and contribution terms are being established by the founders. Until those terms are published, please don't assume that submitting something automatically transfers ownership or grants the project permission to redistribute or modify it.

We will make the applicable licensing terms clear before incorporating contributions into the project.

## Community Expectations

BikeTag is built around a shared love of bicycles, exploration, photography, and community.

We ask everyone participating in this project to:

- Be welcoming and respectful.
- Give constructive feedback.
- Respect different creative approaches and perspectives.
- Be open to collaboration and experimentation.
- Remember that everyone contributes in different ways.

You don't have to agree with every idea. You do need to treat the people sharing them with respect.

## Thank You!

The future of BikeTag should reflect the creativity and character of the communities that play it.

Every sketch, illustration, line of code, suggestion, and thoughtful comment helps us get there.

We're excited to see what we can build together.
